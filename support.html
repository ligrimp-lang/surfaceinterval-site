Common questions

How long does a reel take?

It depends on how much footage there is, how long the reel is, and where the videos are. Footage already on the phone is quickest. Footage still in iCloud has to be streamed over your connection first, and on a slow connection that is the whole of the wait — the app tells you when it is waiting on iCloud rather than on the phone.

Why is a video marked "in iCloud"?

Because the full-size original is not on the phone; iOS keeps only a small copy to save space. The app streams what it needs rather than downloading the whole 4K file, so a reel still works. If you want it faster, open the video in Photos once on Wi-Fi so iOS keeps the original on the phone.

Can I mix vertical and horizontal video in one reel?

No, deliberately. A reel that mixes the two has to either crop your footage or put bars down the side of half of it, and neither is worth doing to a dive. The app keeps vertical and horizontal footage in separate folders and makes a reel of one or the other.

My footage is already colour-corrected in the camera.

The app recognises that and leaves it alone rather than correcting it twice. Osmo Action and Insta360 footage exported with the camera's own correction is the usual case.

Which cameras work?

Any camera that writes an ordinary video file: DJI Osmo Action, GoPro, Insta360, iPhone and most others. Bring the files in from Photos, or straight off a card through Files with a card reader.

What resolution do reels come out at?

1080p. The correction is done at the resolution the footage was shot, and the finished reel is written at 1080p, which is what the places you post it to accept anyway.

The reel stopped halfway.

Reels are made in the foreground, and a phone that gets hot will pause the work and take it up again when it cools. Leaving the app pauses it too. When you come back it carries on from the shot it had reached rather than starting over.

Why is there a watermark?

This first version is free, and reels carry a small mark in the corner. Pricing comes later.

Where does the music come from?

Twenty-eight tracks are built into the app, from Pixabay under its Content License, and one is picked to suit each reel. There is nothing to credit and nothing to pay.

Does the app upload my footage?

No. It makes no network call of its own. See the privacy policy.
